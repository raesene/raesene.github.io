I did a presentation recently talking about the value of security testing, most of which focused on getting the best out of a pen. test, so thought it would be worth noting...
Scoping is, in my experience, a commonly under appreciated part of the penetration testing process in terms of how much time is spent on it. A badly scoped test is likely to have a limited benefit to the client even if the test itself is well executed, as it may not test the right scenarios, look at the right systems or focus on the right areas.
So, there's a couple of different aspects to consider when scoping the test properly

  • What are you looking to prove?
  • What type of testing should be done?
  • Who do you think will attack you?
  • How far should the testing go?

The first one is working out what's in scope of the test. Ideally what you'll be looking to do is confirm the operation of specific controls which you believe to be in place. Some expamples would be

  • Confirming the level of controls from attackers on the Internet attempting to get access to internal resources.
  • Confirming the application security on a new or existing system.
  • Confirming that a wireless installation only allows access to appropriately authorised people

Each of these could form the starting point for a scope for a penetration test.
Once that's done the next thing is to consider, what type of testing will be done. For things like the external test, it's easy to say "well it's an external test, so give the testers the IP range and away they go!", but that doesn't really simulate what is likely to happen in reality.
To achieve the test goals, you need to carry out the same kind of attacks as a real attacker would, so for external testing there are several areas that could be covered.
